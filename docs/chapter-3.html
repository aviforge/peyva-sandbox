Chapter 3

Across the Wire (Networking)

A message reaches someone when you know where to go, which door to use, and how the package travels.

Foundations Beginner 15 min

1. Intuition

  • Chapter 2 got peyva listening on your own machine, but Alice and Bob aren't on the same one.
  • Reaching another machine needs an address (IP), a door (port), a shared language (protocol), and packets to carry the message.
  • The trip itself is unreliable. Packets take different routes, arrive out of order, or vanish, so something has to notice what went missing and ask for it again.

2. Concepts

  • IP Address. Where a machine lives on the network, the equivalent of a street address.
  • Port. Which door on that machine to use: same idea as Chapter 2, reached remotely.
  • Protocol. The agreed-upon way of talking. peyva speaks TCP/IP.
  • Packets. Chunks a message is split into for the trip, reassembled on arrival.

3. Under the Hood

  • Your App -> TCP/IP -> Internet -> the other machine's App, following IP addresses on both ends.
  • TCP re-sends anything lost and reassembles what arrives out of order, so peyva never sees a half-delivered request.
  • The listener from Chapter 2 already speaks TCP/IP. This chapter is about reaching a different machine, not just your own.

5. Break It Test it

  • Networks aren't perfect. See what happens when the path breaks.
  • Turn off Wi-Fi on the device trying to reach peyva. The connection fails immediately with no route to the machine.
  • Try reaching peyva at the wrong port (e.g. 9311 instead of 9310): connection refused, even though the IP address is right.
  • The IP gets you to the right machine, but the port still decides whether anyone answers.

Quick tip: TCP hands your program a complete, in-order stream or fails the connection. It never delivers half a request.
